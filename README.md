# Bank Customer Churn Analysis

I analyzed 10,000 bank customers to find out why people were leaving. Overall churn was 20%, but Germany stood out at almost double that — even though German customers had higher balances and similar activity levels. Built in Excel and Power BI to find the real reason behind the numbers.

## Key Findings

- Overall churn rate: **20.37%**
- Germany's churn rate is roughly **2x** that of France and Spain
- Active membership rates are similar across all three countries, ruling out engagement as the cause
- Germany's customers have **higher average balances** than France and Spain (confirmed using both mean and median, so it's not skewed by outliers)
- This suggests Germany's churn isn't about weaker or less engaged customers — it's likely due to something else, like competition from other banks or dissatisfaction with the service in that market

## Tools Used

- Excel (data cleaning, PivotTables, exploration)
- Power BI (dashboard and visuals)

## Dataset

10,000-row bank customer dataset, commonly used for churn analysis practice. Columns include credit score, geography, gender, age, balance, number of products, activity status, and whether the customer left the bank.
